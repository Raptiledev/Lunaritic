02 — The site

The south pole hides behind its own ground.

The Sun and Earth stay low. A ridge a few kilometres away can decide whether a crew has light, and whether Earth is in view.

03 — The console

One tile. One horizon. One place to read it.

LUNARIS stands on the NASA LOLA Site01 tile, builds the terrain horizon, and compares it with JPL directions for the Sun and Earth.

Site01 · Connecting Ridge

16 km × 16 km · 5 metres · south polar stereographic

  • Explore elevation, slope, roughness, hazard class
  • Window sunlight, longest darkness, Earth in view
  • Compare up to six candidates, then move the landing day
  • Report timeline, 3D site, printable mission summary

04 — The decision

Eligible first. Ranked second.

Gates · team illustration

  • 70% of the window in sunlight
  • 48 h longest darkness, at most
  • 20% of the window with Earth in view
  • 80% favorable ground within 100 m

Weights · sum to 1

  • Sunlight0.35
  • Darkness0.25
  • Earth0.20
  • Terrain0.20

Each contribution is weight × that factor’s score, from 0 to 1. The total summarises geometry for the chosen window. These gates are the team’s illustration for the challenge.
