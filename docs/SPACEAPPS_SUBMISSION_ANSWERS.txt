========================================================================
1. WHAT THE PROJECT IS
========================================================================

LUNARIS (Lunar Navigation, Illumination, and Risk Intelligence System) is a
terrain-aware landing-site tool for the lunar south pole.

It answers, for a point on the NASA LOLA Site01 tile ("Connecting ridge"):

  - How steep and rough is the ground, and how well was it actually measured?
  - Where does the local terrain block the sky?
  - During a chosen mission window, how much of the Sun is above that
    horizon, how long is the longest darkness, and is Earth above the horizon?
  - Which nearby spots and which landing days meet the team's own gates?

It is not an official NASA tool and it does not certify a landing site.
Sunlight is geometry, not electrical power. Earth above the horizon is not a
radio link budget. Scores are weighted geometry, not a probability of
mission success. Hazard thresholds (5 degrees / 10 degrees slope,
0.25 m / 0.5 m roughness) are illustrative, not NASA requirements.

NASA and JPL products the solution does use
--------------------------------------------

A. LRO LOLA Site01, 5 metres per pixel (the science terrain)
  Provider: NASA Planetary Geodynamics Data Archive (PGDA) / LRO LOLA,
            Barker et al. 2021, doi:10.1016/j.pss.2020.105119.
  Page:  https://core2.gsfc.nasa.gov/PGDA/LOLA_5mpp/
  Files: https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/LOLA_5mpp/Site01/
  Tile:  Site01, "Connecting ridge", 16 km by 16 km, 3200 by 3200 pixels.
         South polar stereographic metres on a Moon sphere of radius
         1737.4 km (lat_0 = -90, lon_0 = 0). This is the MOON_ME frame of
         the DE421 ephemeris, which is why the kernels below are also DE421.
  The app downloads these on first use into backend/app/data/dem/ when
  remote data is enabled. They are not committed to git.

  Site01_final_adj_5mpp_surf.tif
      Surface height, metres.
      Used for slope (Horn 3x3), aspect, hillshade, roughness (local-plane
      residual), relief, the terrain horizon, and the 3D site mesh.

  Site01_final_adj_5mpp_ldec.tif
      Count of LOLA laser returns in each pixel.
      Used as confidence: a count above zero is a real measurement; the
      other heights are the published interpolation.

  Site01_final_adj_5mpp_toterr.tif
      Total height RMS error, metres. Shown as height uncertainty.

  Site01_final_adj_5mpp_slperr.tif
      Slope RMS error, degrees. Can keep a pixel out of a hazard class
      when the slope is too uncertain.

  Site01_final_adj_5mpp_slp.tif
      NASA's own slope map, degrees.
      Validation only: compared with our Horn slope. Not used to score a site.

  Not used, though named in the dataset registry as a candidate:
      the wider south-polar LOLA grid (about 80 degrees south).
      The PGDA average-illumination maps (Mazarico et al.) are listed as a
      future check and have not been downloaded.


B. JPL NAIF SPICE kernels (the live Sun and Earth positions)
  Provider: NASA JPL NAIF, generic kernels.
  Root: https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/
  Downloaded on first use into backend/app/data/kernels/. Not in git.
  Coverage is 1900 to 2050.

  naif0012.tls
      Leap seconds, so a UTC mission time is the time SPICE expects.
      .../lsk/naif0012.tls

  pck00011.tpc
      Planetary constants.
      .../pck/pck00011.tpc

  moon_080317.tf and moon_assoc_me.tf
      Frame definitions for the lunar Mean Earth / polar-axis frame.
      .../fk/satellites/moon_080317.tf
      .../fk/satellites/moon_assoc_me.tf

  moon_pa_de421_1900-2050.bpc
      Moon orientation for DE421, 1900–2050.
      .../pck/moon_pa_de421_1900-2050.bpc

  de421.bsp
      Positions of the Sun, Earth, and Moon.
      .../spk/planets/a_old_versions/de421.bsp

  Why DE421 and not the Horizons DE441 file: the LOLA tile is referenced to
  DE421's MOON_ME frame. Using that same frame means a DEM coordinate and a
  sky direction are in one system.

  What the code does with them: SPICE spkcpo returns the apparent direction
  from a fixed surface point to the Sun or the Earth (light time and stellar
  aberration; the Moon has no atmosphere, so no refraction). That vector is
  turned into azimuth clockwise from lunar north and elevation above the
  local horizontal of the 1737.4 km sphere. The apparent disk uses the IAU
  nominal solar radius 695,700 km and the WGS84 Earth equatorial radius
  6,378.137 km. Those two radii are published constants, not kernel files.

  Why SPICE for the live calculation, and Horizons for the check: Horizons
  is the right independent reference for a sample of times. SPICE lets the
  same geometry run for every time step, every landing day, and every 100 m
  cell without calling Horizons once per cell.


C. Numbers that are not downloaded files
  Moon radius 1,737,400 m, taken from the CRS of the LOLA GeoTIFFs.
  IAU 2015 nominal solar radius and the WGS84 Earth equatorial radius, used
  only to draw the apparent disks.

Why this set, in one paragraph
------------------------------
The south pole is lit or dark because of local relief, not because of a
global average. A 5 m LOLA tile is small enough to see a ridge and large
enough to cast horizon rays. SPICE on DE421 puts the Sun and Earth in that
tile's own frame. Horizons, from the challenge resource list, is the check
that those angles are right. Trek, also from that list, shows the site on
the globe without being mixed into the numbers. Quickmap and the CSA portal
were left unused so every number on screen comes from a file the program
opens, not from a screenshot of another website.

5. IDEA AND STRUCTURE (OURS, NOT THE TOOL'S)
========================================================================

Idea, Team Alpha 8
  At the south pole the Sun and Earth stay near the horizon, so a ridge a
  few kilometres away can decide sunlight and a direct view of Earth. The
  idea is to stand on a real LOLA surface, compute that local horizon, and
  compare it with JPL Sun and Earth directions over the dates a crew might
  land. The result should stay explainable: show the terrain, the sky, and
  the gates, and refuse to show a number the data cannot support.

Structure of the program, Team Alpha 8
  backend/   calculation service
             terrain load, slope and hazard, horizon rays, SPICE ephemeris,
             visibility over time, calendar, maps, explainable score,
             Horizons comparison.
  frontend/  the console a person uses
             dashboard, explorer, analysis, slip planner, comparison,
             validation, 3D view, methodology, report.
  data       NASA and JPL files downloaded on use, kept out of git.
             The dataset registry records provider, role, frame, and units.
             Unknown fields stay empty rather than being filled in.

The coding assistant followed that structure. It did not replace it.
